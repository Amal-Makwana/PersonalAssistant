1) Vision, Mission, and Strategic Outcomes

The product vision is trust-centric automation: convert event-oriented inbox signals into dependable reminders so users no longer fail between “email received” and “reminder configured.”

Strategic Narrative + Mission

  • Close the fragile manual gap between inbox events and reminder setup.
  • Deliver consistent outcomes through authorized inbox ingestion and controlled automation.
  • Mission: enable users to act on commitments by automatically transforming event emails into reminders.

Horizon Planning

  • Near term (V1): Gmail ingestion, extraction, scheduling, and Google Calendar sync.
  • Mid term: richer reminder logic, broader providers, more context intelligence.
  • Long term: broader inbox-to-action assistant capabilities.
MVP boundary from vision: WhatsApp and SMS are explicitly deferred to post-MVP while core email-to-calendar reliability is established.

2) Product Requirements Baseline (PRD)

requirements.md is the authoritative PRD and consolidates business requirements, functional and non-functional expectations, delivery governance, and risk/compliance constraints. business-requirements.md now serves as a consolidation notice pointing back to that single baseline.

Functional Contract (FR)

  • FR-01..FR-03: secure onboarding, consent clarity, and event email detection.
  • FR-04..FR-06: extraction quality, event persistence, and reminder schedule generation.
  • FR-09 + FR-10: Google Calendar sync and duplicate prevention are MVP critical.
  • FR-07/FR-08 are defined as future-phase messaging channels.
  • FR-11 captures preference setup and configuration behavior.

Quality + Operational Expectations

  • Extraction quality threshold and low-confidence handling rules are part of product quality gates.
  • Calendar sync includes performance and reliability expectations with explicit failure handling.
  • Reliability, observability, security/privacy, and supportability are treated as first-class requirements.
  • Delivery readiness depends on measurable criteria, not feature completion alone.

3) MVP Scope and Boundary Decisions

In Scope (MVP)

  • Google authentication and Gmail permissioned connection.
  • Event email detection, detail extraction, persistence, and deduplication controls.
  • Automatic default reminder schedule generation.
  • Google Calendar synchronization as a committed MVP feature.

Out of Scope (MVP)

  • WhatsApp and SMS delivery channels remain post-MVP.
  • Advanced multi-provider integrations outside MVP baseline.
  • Non-essential expansion features that dilute core reliability outcomes.
Release readiness condition: MVP release gates require reliable end-to-end execution from ingestion through calendar sync, with duplicates prevented and expected reminders generated.
Prototype persistence status: the Events vertical slice now treats all event APIs (GET /events, GET /events/:id, POST /events, PUT /events/:id/reminder-plan, GET /events/:id/notification-history) as DB-backed while preserving frontend response contracts.

8) Risks, Constraints, Assumptions, and Open Items

Top Delivery Risks

  • Extraction quality degradation causing incorrect reminder timing.
  • Calendar provider transient/terminal failure paths reducing trust if not observable/remediable.
  • Duplicate normalization misses causing repeated reminders.

Constraints and Assumptions

  • MVP channel boundary is strict: no active WhatsApp/SMS runtime path.
  • Google ecosystem integration is the MVP identity + calendar backbone.
  • Release confidence depends on acceptance evidence and traceability completeness.
